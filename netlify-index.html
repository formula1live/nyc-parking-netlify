Where to Park in NYC Without Overpaying: 2026 Prices by Borough

Driving into New York does not have to mean paying the first garage price you see. Rates change sharply from block to block and borough to borough, and a five-minute walk can cut an evening of parking from over $80 in Midtown to under $25 in parts of Brooklyn or Queens.

Start with the live rates: the regularly updated NYC parking garage rates 2026 guide on NYC Parkings lists current prices by neighborhood, with no booking fees and no sign-up.

Manhattan vs. the outer boroughs

Manhattan is the expensive outlier. Monthly parking there commonly runs $500 to $1,200 or more, and event nights near Times Square, Madison Square Garden and Broadway theatres push hourly prices to their highest. Brooklyn garages near Downtown and Williamsburg tend to price noticeably lower, Queens options near Long Island City and Astoria lower still, and metered street parking in the outer boroughs is typically $1.50–$2.50 for the first hour, compared with $5.00–$5.50 below 96th Street in Manhattan.

Three ways drivers overpay

First, the Manhattan parking tax: garages add 18.375% on top of the posted rate, although Manhattan residents can apply for an exemption that brings it down to 10.375%. Second, surge pricing on game and concert nights — arriving an hour earlier or parking a few blocks from the venue avoids most of it. Third, ignoring the meter rules: metered parking is free on Sundays and major legal holidays unless a posted sign says otherwise, and alternate side parking suspensions are announced by NYC DOT throughout the year.

A simple plan before you leave

Pick your neighborhood first, compare two or three garages there rather than the single closest one, and check whether a weekend or evening flat rate beats the hourly meter. Overnight parking usually falls between $37 and $60 outside the priciest Midtown and Financial District blocks.
